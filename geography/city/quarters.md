# Quarters & Architects

Atlantis is divided into quarters — distinct districts, each shaped by the creative vision of a lead **Architect**. An Architect has near-total control over the look, feel, and atmosphere of their quarter: its buildings, streets, materials, lighting, even its weather-within-weather. No two quarters need resemble each other, and many don't.

## Material Freedom

Because materials are abundant (see [Wealth & Abundance](../../society/wealth-and-abundance.md)) and matter itself can be synthesized from the city's fusion reactors (see [Energy Supply](../../systems/technology/energy-supply.md)), Architects are rarely constrained by cost or scarcity. If a vision calls for marble, or timber, or something stranger, it can generally be built. The limiting factor on a quarter's design is imagination and taste — not budget.

## Dust as a Design Tool

Architects don't just build structures — they direct atmosphere. The city's [ubiquitous particle dust](../../systems/technology/ubiquitous-particle-dust.md) can be tuned locally within a quarter to reinforce whatever mood or era the Architect is evoking: rolling fog, a particular quality of light, a chill in the air, even faint ambient sound. A quarter styled after 1800s London, for instance, might have the dust lay in a permanent low fog and dampen the light to a gaslamp haze — turning a themed backdrop into something closer to a fully inhabited experience.

## How Architects Work

An Architect is not a draftsman in the traditional sense — nobody is sketching blueprints or laying bricks by hand. The role is closer to a creative director: an Architect describes a vision, reacts to what comes back, and refines it through conversation and feedback with an AI system that handles the actual technical realization — structure, materials, logistics, dust behavior.

This is the same intention-and-execution pattern used elsewhere in the city (see [Weather Control](../../systems/technology/weather-control.md) and [AI Infrastructure](../../systems/ai-infrastructure.md)): the human supplies the taste and the direction, the AI supplies the complexity.

## Hierarchy: Lead and Sub-Architects

A quarter is rarely one person's work alone. The lead Architect owns and drives the overall vision, but sub-architects are entrusted with sub-areas — a street, a block, a district-within-the-district — where they work the same conversational, AI-directed way, in service of the lead's overall vision rather than their own. Rising from sub-architect to lead Architect of one's own quarter is itself a step on the same prestige ladder.

## Becoming an Architect

Architects are not appointed by wealth or bought into the role. In keeping with the rest of Atlantis's culture (see [Arts & Style](../../society/arts-and-style.md)), the position is earned through prestige — recognition from others for one's creative work and taste. Designers climb the ranks by having their creations admired, discussed, and followed.

Assignment is governed by the **Architect Guild** — a self-governing, peer-run body rather than a corporate or state office. The Guild recognizes acclaim and formally entrusts designers with quarters or sub-areas; the more acclaimed a designer, the more (or larger) territory they're trusted to shape.

## Boundaries, Popularity & Vacancy

Quarter boundaries aren't fixed by decree — they're organic and fuzzy, and they move with where people actually choose to live. Housing and space are abundant, so citizens are free to live wherever suits them, and they will often tolerate real inconvenience — fewer amenities, less practical layouts — to live somewhere stylish. Being "in" outweighs comfort.

This gives the beauty-versus-livability tension a natural check: if a quarter becomes impractical without being compelling enough to justify it, people simply leave. No guild or authority needs to intervene — the quarter's footprint contracts on its own, and the Architect's governed territory (and standing) shrinks with it.

If a quarter empties out faster than its Architect can reinvent it, and the Architect no longer has the drive to keep pushing the vision, the quarter becomes effectively unclaimed — free for an aspiring Architect to stake a claim, typically with the Guild ratifying the handover. New, never-before-shaped territory starts out unclaimed the same way, waiting for someone to stake it.
